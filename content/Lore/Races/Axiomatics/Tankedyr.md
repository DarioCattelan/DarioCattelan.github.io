# Overview

*Pathfinder possible ancestries:* Kholo, Catfolk, Ratfolk, Vanara, Minotaur, Tengu,  Kobolds

Tankedyr are a race of animal-like [[axiomatic mortals]] created by [[Graedor The Merchant]]. Like [[Fey Folk]], their appearance varies greatly, but they are fundamentally a single species.

Tankedyr are prone to intense natural urges, and are more intense in their cravings and emotions than other races.

Tankedyr have no national identity, and are organized in a series of free mercantile city states.

Tankedyr worship [[Graedor The Merchant]] in a monotheistic religion which promises eternal comfort in the afterlife to the faithful.


# Identity

## Homeland
Tankedyr live in a series of mercantile city states, called [[the Kaupangsborg]] located in [[Audland]]. The Kaupangsborg are governed by proto-capitalist plutocracy in the form of a council of merchant princes in each city, with the exception of the holy city of [[Goadheimr]], which is ruled by plutocratic capitalist theocracy.

The member states of [[the Kaupangsborg]] have dynamic, fragmented, and competitive relationships, forming and breaking alliances and rivalries, sometimes even going to war against one another. Different city states have on occasion formed leagues to bolster their influence in the region or ward off invaders, sometimes forcing other cities to join under threat of attack. Despite the fragmented nature of [[the Kaupangsborg]], the city states share a cultural identity through ancestry and religion, which sometimes unites them against external threats.

### Born of beasts
Tankedyr believe that their people were created last of all [[axiomatic mortals]]. Legend holds that after the world was full of life, [[Graedor The Merchant]], the only [[The Fundamentals|Fundamental]] who had not made a people to live amongst, saw the hardship endured by the creatures of land and sky, and wept. From his tears sprouted [[Audland]], and when the creatures of [[Kaloshen]] ate and drank of that land, they knew his love, and learned the arts of speech and thought.

### Truhuggun
[[Truhuggun]] is the state religion of all members of [[the Kaupangsborg]]. Dubbed "the faith of comfort", it is a monotheistic [[axiomatist]]  religion that preaches the maximizing of contentment and rewards worshipers with a permanent, idyllic afterlife. Followers of [[Truhuggun]] believe that the ideal life is one which created an abundance of luxury and ease, and all the comfort (for oneself or others) that was the result of ones actions will be delivered back unto the faithful forever in the [[Truhuggun#The Halls of Silk|the Halls of Silk]]. The faith of [[Truhuggun]] is lead by the [[Truhuggun#The Chantry of Truhuggun|the Chantry of Truhuggun]], a religious establishment.

### The Chantry and Asmenn
Every city state in [[the Kaupangsborg]] has multiple churches and cathedrals of [[Truhuggun#The Chantry of Truhuggun|the Chantry of Truhuggun]]. Once a week, faithful [[Tankedyr]] are called to their communal place of worship to attend the [[Truhuggun#Asmenn|Asmenn]]. During the Asmenn, worshippers listen to liturgical songs, donate to the Chantry, and share a communal meal.

### Sannlif
Sannlif (san-LEEF) (the Honest Life in [[Common]]) is the Tankedyr belief that in the pursuit of maximizing ones personal comfort as part of [[Truhuggun]], one must not minimize worldly comfort. In other words, it is a sin to purposefully swindle, cheat, or otherwise cause another creature pain disproportionate to the pleasure being added to ones own life. In order to maintain sannlif, wealthy Tankedyr are encouraged to donate regularly to charity through [[Truhuggun#The Chantry of Truhuggun|The Chantry of Truhuggun]].

### Mundane spirits
Unlike most cultures in [[Kaloshen]], Tankedyr do not typically assign any sacred or divine nature to [[Spirits]], considering them simply another kind of creature in their magical world. There are no shrines or offerings given to [[Spirits]] in [[the Kaupangsborg]], and [[Spirits]] are often less present in the city states than in other urban environments.

### Devils and Pacts
Although Tankedyr don't typically worship [[Spirits]], they do still interact with them in their day to day lives. Most notably, unlike many cultures which shun [[Devils]], [[Tankedyr]] have no such taboo for pacts made with spirit-folk. Quite the opposite, Tankedyr tend to flaunt their pact-enhanced [[Breath]], and although the nature of a pact is private (often by magically compelled necessity), Tankedyr have mastered the art of negotiating just enough leeway in the small print to allow public flaunting of their business acumen.

## Language
Tankedyr read, write, and speak [[Kaupmal]], a tonal, rhythmic language with much nuance in the imperative tense, and beautiful application to song and poetry.

## Organization
The Tankedyr are culturally organized into city states, called [[the Kaupangsborg]], each led by a proto-capitalist plutocracy of merchant princes, with the exception of [[Goadheimr]] which is a religious theocracy and the "first amongst equals" of the free states. Each plutocratic city state of [[the Kaupangsborg]] has slightly different organization, tat being said, nearly all share some relatively universal features.

### Merchant Councils
Every city state in [[the Kaupangsborg]] has a council of wealthy merchant houses who earn their place due to the size of their accounts and businesses. Merchants must be invited into the council of a city state, but such invitation becomes a necessity once the business of a merchant grows large enough. Members of the merchant councils typically start as successful guilds, banking families, trading cartels, or caravan syndicates who have grown so large that their influence over the city state and its people cannot be ignored. It is of great benefit for the members of the merchant council to have a forum for collaboration, as they can divvy-up markets, artificially control the costs of goods and services, and avoid violence that would impact profits.

In contrast to the monarchies of the [[Dwarves]] and [[Fey Folk]], the merchant councils of [[the Kaupangsborg]] govern through contracts in place of laws. Contracts are proposed and voted on by members of the council, and are more negotiable than feudal laws. Public offices are elected by the merchant council, and have set tenures, and guilds are leveraged to set standards and enforce contracts. Armed forces, such as city guards, armies, and navies are privately owned mercenary bands, paid for and controlled by individual members of the council

### The Pashas and Princes
Most of the merchant councils of [[the Kaupangsborg]] have an elected head, called a Pasha or Prince. The Pasha is selected from among the members of the council and represent the city state to foreign powers. The Pasha is a plenipotentiary, meaning they have authority to make legally binding contracts and agreements with foreign states without ratification by their council, but it is expected that any significant decision be discussed and agreed upon by the city state council before ultimate action is taken, as the Pasha could always be removed by a vote of no-confidence.

The position of Pasha is not always a boon to a member of the council, as it sometimes necessitates long absences from the management of their accounts. It is not unheard of for a council member to be forced into the position of Pasha by the rest of the council.

### Heralds
Two things distinguish the Herald of [[Graedor The Merchant]] from other Heralds. Firstly, there is not always a Herald of [[Graedor The Merchant|Graedor]], and the position often remains vacant for many years, sometimes decades. Secondly, [[Graedor The Merchant]] does not chose his own Herald, instead, it is almost as if the power of the Herald choses its own bearer.

The Heralds of Graedor are prophets, clerics, philosophers, and champions of the tenets of [[Truhuggun]], but *not* necessarily of [[Truhuggun#The Chantry of Truhuggun|The Chantry]]. They appear in times of great hardship for the Tankedyr and the people of [[Kaloshen]], and are balms to the weak and vulnerable during plagues, wars, and disasters. As a result, Heralds of [[Graedor The Merchant]] are almost universally revered by all [[axiomatic mortals]].

The Heralds of the Tankedyr do not hold any legal or formal political power in [[the Kaupangsborg]], but their teachings have lead to religious and political reform in the past.

# Biology
Tankedyr have a diverse physiology that makes them appear as if they were a variety of species, like the [[Fey Folk]], but are in fact all a single species. Most Tankedyr are visibly mammalian, but some are more avian in appearance, and a small number appear reptilian. All Tankedyr, even those that appear similar to herbivores, are carnivorous opportunistic omnivores.

## Salhungr
Salhungr (SAHL-hoon-gur) is an instinctual hunger that possesses Tankedyr on a weekly basis. Salhungr can be near overpowering, and Tankedyr children are taught from a young age the exercises to control this craving. If salhungr strikes when a Tankedyr is in a weakened mental state, it can lead them to strike out violently in the pursuit of meat. Luckily, regularly overeating around once a week can suppress salhungr.

# Relationships

Tankedyr have relatively good relationships with all [[axiomatic mortals]] due to their role as traders and merchants.

That being said, some city states in [[the Kaupangsborg]] are still formally at war with [[Orlins]], having never signed [[The Freewalk Accords]], and have large populations of indentured servants.
