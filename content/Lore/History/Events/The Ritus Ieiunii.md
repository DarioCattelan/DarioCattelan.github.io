# Overview
The Rite of Fasting was an event during the [[Age of Witches]] about the discovery of alcoholic spirits. It is passed on via oral tradition in the [[Truhuggun]] faith as a cautionary tale.

# Synopsis
[[Graedor The Merchant]] hungers for a new taste, one he has never had before that will aid him in a long hibernation. A great [[Truhuggun#Asmenn|Asmenn]] feast is to be held in two weeks time from the announcement, where Graedor will judge all the foods brought before him. Many worthy [[Tankedyr]] search the world for a meal rich and calming enough to aid their god in his rest. [[Shrall the Drunk]], a brewer of beer, sought to draw the [[Breath]] out of a beer, and bottle it for Graedor.

Shrall seeks advice from the [[Spirits]] of her field, who aid her in making many strong and powerful beers, but fail at coaxing the Breath of the drink out of the grain. Eventually, Shrall seeks the council of her hearth, Elddis, a fiery spirit who is often resting languidly round the stones of her chimney. Elddis invites Shrall to place her equipment in the fire, so that the spirit can boil the Breath from the brew, but that Shrall must promise to never let her drink the results, even if she begs for it. Shrall promises, and together they succeed in drawing the Breath from the beer, forming a rich colorless alcohol.

Shrall and Elddis bring their new creation to Graedor's feast, and all agree that they are the victor of the challenge. The merriment of the feast attracts all manner of Spirits, who all wish to sample the drink. Elddis and her kin beg Shrall three times to let them taste the brew. Shrall refuses twice, but eventually succumbs to their demands once the hour is late and she is heavily drunk. When the alcohol touches Elddis' lips, she surges with ecstatic power, prompting all the Spirits to drink heavily of the brew. When the morning comes, the Spirits of the feast find themselves [[Spirits#Alcohol Binding|bound]] to the [[Corporeal World]]. In their rage, they curse Shrall for breaking her promise, causing all who drink of a brewer's trade to feel the pain of these bound Spirits the next morning.
