# Overview
The Victoria Viae was a mythological event during the [[Age of Spirits]] where the Nomad defeated the Great Spirit [[Sonokar Chiri]] and seized control of [[the Warrens]].

## Early Conflict
Early in the [[Age of Spirits]] the magical demi-plane byways known as [[the Warrens]] opened throughout the [[Corporeal World]]. Many [[Spirits]] issued forth from the dimensional pathways, possessing mortals and kidnapping them into the twisting depths. [[the Warrens]] spread all across [[Kaloshen]], making it impossible to predict where and when great spirits would appear. Seemingly at all entrances to [[the Warrens]] stood a powerful [[Spirits#Chiri|Chiri]] by the name [[Sonokar Chiri|Sonokar]], whose manifestation was [[the Warrens]] itself, and whose [[Breath]] was unmatched along the magical roads. The power and convenience of [[the Warrens]] was not lost on [[The Fundamentals]], and they waged several battles for control of [[the Warrens]] against [[Sonokar Chiri]] and each other for control.

## Fable of Sonokar Chiri
There came a time when [[The Fundamentals|the masters of Kaloshen]] had fierce and mighty aims upon [[the Warrens|the highway behind the world]], and were locked in terrible opposition with the [[Spirits#Chikara (Great Spirits)|Chikara]] [[Sonokar Chiri]]. Many worthy hosts of the gods were rebuffed, and many Herald fell within the winding depths, but none was more insistent and cunning than [[The Nomad]]. Season upon season he and his [[Heralds]] made a great show of arms and delved deeply into [[the Warrens]], only to be rebuffed by [[Sonokar Chiri]]. Many kinsmen of the Nomad were lost, and one such was Alburh, daughter of the Herald [[Aldgift Heralda]].

Sorrowful at the loss of her child, [[Aldgift Heralda]] sought augury with [[The Nomad|the Tale Master]] as to a solution. A mighty bird of span six and twenty hands was captured and its entrails read, and the Nomad foretold of a tale. In the home of Aldgift there would be told a tale so rich with [[Breath]] that it would send [[Sonokar Chiri]] into an endless sleep as deep and immovable as stone. A great and trembling anticipation arose in Aldgift, and the Herald readied her household for a festival of tales, summoning all raconteurs, skalds, poets, historians, playwrights, authors, and bards to come and perform their novel work.

Many came to the home of [[Aldgift Heralda|Aldgift]], and for four days and five nights she heard the tales of travellers and artists, warriors and kings, spirits and gods, but none told a story that stirred her [[Breath]]. Small grew the larders of [[Aldgift Heralda]] over those nights, and it came to pass that all tales had been told and all there was to show was an empty cellar. Tired grew her household, and she dismissed the storymen and women of the festival, retiring to her chambers with her children.

Great excitement had the festival brought to her young kin, and Aldgift found that her youngest daughter, [[Eadgift Wicca]], was of mirth and merriment, and not at all prepared for repose. So it came to pass that Aldgift summoned the child's nurse, a wisewoman of the village, to prepare her for bed, and under the hearth of the nursery, the wise woman told a lullaby for the child. The tale was an adventure of a young girl, much alike with [[Eadgift Wicca|Eadgift]], who would travel deep into a magical kingdom and face a horrible Spirit King to save her kin from his clutches by turning him to stone. And indeed, this tale was the tale that [[Aldgift Heralda]] had sought, and there was a great wind of [[Breath]] within the nursery, and a deep [[Arcana]] persisted about, and all but [[Eadgift Wicca]] fell into a deep slumber.

And there came to the doorway [[The Nomad]], dressed in a motley of black and white, and took with him all the kinsmen of the house who had been enchanted, so as to guard them in their slumber. And to Eadgift he spoke thus, that she was now his Herald, and that to lift the curse she must make her way to the kingdom of her story, and face the Great Spirit who held dominion there, and trade his slumber for that of her folk. 

With great haste did [[Eadgift Wicca]] make her journey to the entrance of [[the Warrens]], and before its guardian did she stand. Endlessly long was he, with many hundreds of hands and scales of azure and gold. Teeth bore he, so long and sharp as the bitter wind, and his eyes were as like sunken pits of fire. Under his belly was the great horde of all that he stole, and in a gilded cage of cold iron was Eadgift's sister. Captured was Eadgift, for greatly outmatched was she in strength of arms to [[Sonokar Chiri]], and in the very same cage was she placed with her sister. Joyful were the sisters at their reunion, but a fleeting joy it was, for [[Sonokar Chiri]] soon spoke to them, and told them that as he had taken all they had to share, he would now make a meal of their [[Breath]].

But cunning was [[Eadgift Wicca]], and so she lamented to [[Sonokar Chiri]] the terrible shame it was that he would never know the great riches she yet possessed. Full of avarice was he, and demanded she relinquish all that she had to his horde. It was not an object, countered Eadgift, but a tale, a tale so valuable that the Herald of [[The Nomad]] herself had searched the world over to find it, but she would yet give it to Sonokar if he would but release her from this cage so that she may whisper it in his ear.

Swayed was he, for careful had been Eadgift with her words, and not a lie had she told the spirit. And so did she tell the tale to [[Sonokar Chiri]], and a mire of lethargy descended upon him, and so deeply did he sleep that his [[Breath]] stilled and turned to stone.

Down the road did ride [[the Nomad]], and all arrayed behind him paraded Eadgift's kin awakened, and a kingdom did he claim within [[the Warrens]], and to the keeping of the [[Sonokar Stone]] did he give [[Eadgift Wicca]], and all was well in the world once again.

# Outcomes and Impacts

- [[The Nomad]] gained control of [[the Warrens]]
- [[Sonokar Chiri]] was trapped within the [[Sonokar Stone]]
