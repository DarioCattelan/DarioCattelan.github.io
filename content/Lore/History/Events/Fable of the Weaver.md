# Overview

The Fable of the Weaver is a myth originally written in [[Elvish]] from the [[Age of Gentle Waters]]. It is a moral story about patience and respect for the cycles, and is referenced in the [[Nihir Lawall]]. It is one of the oldest surviving written myths in [[Kaloshen]].

## the Fable

There was a time when death was still novel to Elves, and brought with it a great time of sorrow as children became few and far between. So it came to pass that the wife of the weaver [[Nalu Ipsin]] was struct down with illness, and had a flux of death about her, and all the people of the town showed such lamenting as the streets were filled with wails. And the wails caused much distress to the fair weavers wife, and she would not let her lover part from her bed for 10 days and 10 nights, whereupon she said she could not abide the fear of being apart for so long, and that they must promise to sequester her from the Breath of death[^1] after her passing.

Thereupon did Nalu promise unto her a pact, that they would travel far afield to the borders of death and retrieve her from the holds of the underworld. Not but that very moment did the goodwife's [[Breath]] depart her, and it was said to have flown from her breast as like the shutters of a window being blown apart by a mighty storm, and her death was a death of repose and contentment upon her face.

So it came to pass that Nalu departed the bed of their beloved's death, taking with them their livelihood upon their back. Across the face of Kaloshen did they travel, trading away all of that which they carried save for the fine shears and sharp needle of their trade, until finally they arrived at the place where the dead collected. Many souls did wait at the gates of life to be judged by [[Elenwe The Farmer|the High Tari]], such that Nalu could not find their beloved in the field of pilgrims.

Supplicating to him for their return, the pilgrims of the dead espoused their virtue in such clamor that Nalu took pity upon their requests, and answered the voices with their action. Taking from their meager pack their shears, Nalu cut a seam into the barrier of life, and beheld the Breath of souls which issued forth. But merriment gloomed darkly as Nalu witnessed the misshapen corporeal form of the dead as they crossed the barrier in Kaloshen. Unready were they yet for the release of new life, and their wounds from yore pressed upon them into forms both illmade and malevolent.

With such fleetness as befit their trade, Nalu sought to mend the seam they had made in the fabric of life, but was without thread save for their long raven hair. Back and forth, back and forth went Nalu's needle, and what was once in twain met together at the pull of their fine hair, but the spirits of the dead made great force upon the stitch before it could be done, ripping the gap anew. With a great indigo Breath, Nalu pushed themselves into the gap, holding against the exhalation of souls. Taking their needle once more Nalu, sewed themselves into the seam, sequestering the press of the dead forever. There they stand still to this day, warding the gap they rent in the flesh of life.


[^1]: This is believed to be [[The Godwall]]
